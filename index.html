What these numbers mean
Visitors
People who came to the site during the dates picked, each counted once however many times they returned. Google Analytics calls them active users.
New visitors
Visitors on their first ever visit to the site.
Visits
Each time someone comes to the site. One visitor can make several visits; a visit ends after 30 minutes without activity. Google Analytics calls them sessions.
Page views
Every page opened, including the same page opened twice.
Engaged visits
The share of visits that lasted 10 seconds or longer, opened two or more pages, or included a key action. The rest left almost straight away.
Time engaged
On average, how long each visitor had the site open in front of them, across all their visits. Time spent in another tab doesn't count.
Key actions
The actions marked as key events in Google Analytics, such as a contact form sent or a phone number tapped.
Desktop & laptop
Google Analytics puts laptops and desktop computers in one group and can't tell them apart.
Locations
Worked out from each visitor's internet connection, so they're approximate. A VPN or a mobile network can place someone in the wrong city.
Compared with
Every change shown is against the same number of days immediately before the dates picked.

Rolling ranges such as "28 days" end yesterday, as they do in Google Analytics, so the totals match what you'd see there. Today's visits appear under This month and Right now. When Google Signals is on, Google Analytics holds back very small counts to protect privacy, so a few low numbers can be missing.
